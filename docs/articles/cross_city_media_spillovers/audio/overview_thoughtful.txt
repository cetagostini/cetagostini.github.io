(Calm Deep) Let me walk you through a measurement problem that sits quietly inside most multi-market organizations. [pause]

When we build marketing mix models, we typically fit one per city. The assumption is implicit: media spend in Caracas affects outcomes in Caracas. Spend in Valencia affects outcomes in Valencia. [pause] But that assumption is often false.

Cross-city spillover is a form of interference in the causal inference sense. Exposure assigned to one unit affects another unit's outcome. The article approaches this as a Bayesian measurement problem with structural knowledge. [pause] The route mask encodes what the business considers possible. The posterior estimates how large those allowed effects are.

The implementation uses PyMC-Marketing's MuEffect protocol. A custom effect reads the fitted channel contribution tensor, multiplies by a sparse coefficient, and returns a contribution of the right dimension. MaskedPrior ensures only plausible routes are estimated.

The test setup is deliberately simple: two cities, ten channels, three spill routes at ten percent. [pause] The model recovers the direct effects and the spill coefficients. Sampler diagnostics are clean.

This is the simplest version that respects the business structure. It is not the only approach. But it is the right starting point.
