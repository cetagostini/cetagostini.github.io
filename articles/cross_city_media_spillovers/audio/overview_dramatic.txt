(Serious Magnetic) There is a blind spot in how most companies measure marketing. [pause]

Every marketing mix model lives inside its own city border. Facebook spend in Caracas belongs to Caracas. Google spend in Valencia belongs to Valencia. Clean. Tidy. [pause] Wrong.

Media does not respect municipal boundaries. A billboard in one city shifts search behavior in another. A radio campaign reaches listeners across borders. And when the model cannot see that spillover, it does the worst possible thing: [pause] it attributes those orders to the wrong channel in the wrong city.

This article fixes it. [pause] Using PyMC-Marketing, the authors build a custom additive effect that reads the fitted media contribution from a source city and routes a bounded share to a receiver city. A Boolean mask specifies which paths are allowed. A sparse prior ensures only those paths get estimated.

Two synthetic cities. Ten channels. Three known spill routes at ten percent each. [pause] The model recovers all three.

The lesson: [pause] if your campaigns have regional reach, your attribution model needs to account for it. Otherwise you are optimizing based on a map that does not match the territory.
